## 1. Defense for decks that attack

### Takeaway
The sources share one defensive toolkit: deathtouch and reach or flying blockers that make attacks costly, punishment and attack-tax effects, and a few creatures kept home. The advice is qualitative. No source counts defensive pieces for an attacking deck, and none analyzes crack-back or vigilance as a team plan.

### Cited findings
- **Opinion.** The survival rule: a deck should stabilize before it wins with its own plan. The article's advice on when to attack or hold back is thin. ([C2](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01162024-advanced-multiplayer-theory-4-you-cant-win-until-you-dont-lose))
- **Opinion.** Keep flying or reach blockers back. For the fastest decks the rule of thumb is a defender costing three or less with 7 to 8 toughness; examples are a two-mana 0/6 reach Wall and a five-mana 2/7 reach Spider. ([C2](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01162024-advanced-multiplayer-theory-4-you-cant-win-until-you-dont-lose))
- **Example.** A six-mana 3/3 indestructible flash blocker ambushes an attacker, then counterattacks with vigilance and stays untapped for later defense. This is the only vigilance-plus-defense example in the fetched sources. ([C2](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01162024-advanced-multiplayer-theory-4-you-cant-win-until-you-dont-lose))
- **Evidence (rule logic).** A vigilance creature does not tap when it attacks, so effects that destroy tapped creatures cannot take it after an attack; the author says tap-to-destroy effects fail against vigilance. ([C1](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01022024-advanced-multiplayer-strategies-2-the-density-of-creatures)) The same tappers are compared in [C2](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01162024-advanced-multiplayer-theory-4-you-cant-win-until-you-dont-lose): Royal Assassin's tap ability costs no mana, other tappers cost three or four, and Seal of Doom and Executioner's Capsule total about three. The author says these miss vigilance creatures and token hordes.
- **Opinion.** Deathtouch deters, because nobody wants to trade a bigger creature into it. The best deterrents have flying or reach: Baleful Strix (two mana, draws a card) and Vampire Nighthawk. Deadly Recluse (two-mana 1/2 reach) is a cheap example. Typhoid Rats, Acidic Slime, Basilisk Collar and Poison Dart Frog are listed as deathtouch extras. ([C1](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01022024-advanced-multiplayer-strategies-2-the-density-of-creatures); [C2](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01162024-advanced-multiplayer-theory-4-you-cant-win-until-you-dont-lose))
- **Opinion.** Punishers: High Priest of Penance and Black Cat, both two-mana 1/1s, punish damage or death, so opponents avoid attacking them. ([C2](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01162024-advanced-multiplayer-theory-4-you-cant-win-until-you-dont-lose))
- **Opinion.** Rattlesnake: No Mercy (four mana) destroys a creature that deals combat damage to you. The author rates it the best rattlesnake ever printed. ([C2](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01162024-advanced-multiplayer-theory-4-you-cant-win-until-you-dont-lose))
- **Opinion.** Other deterrents named: Maze of Ith and Kor Haven (lands), Horn of Deafening and Tawnos's Coffin (artifacts), Seal of Doom and Seal of Fire (enchantments). Creatures that stop attackers: Lieutenant Kirtar, Songstitcher, Sorceress Queen, Serendib Sorcerer. ([C1](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01022024-advanced-multiplayer-strategies-2-the-density-of-creatures))
- **Opinion.** Brash Taunter draws no attacks at all, the author says. Players often avoid pointless attacks into blockers that cannot die. ([C2](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01162024-advanced-multiplayer-theory-4-you-cant-win-until-you-dont-lose))
- **Opinion (deterrence by appearance).** Opponents judge an attack by how many creatures a player appears to have, not by whether they can block. A player with few creatures becomes the easiest target. In the author's example a hasted, flying Angel did not attack one player, who had no way to block it. ([C1](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01022024-advanced-multiplayer-strategies-2-the-density-of-creatures))
- **Evidence (rule logic).** Unless the defender has flash, nothing can block an attacker aimed at a player with no creatures. An empty board is therefore the safe target. ([C1](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01022024-advanced-multiplayer-strategies-2-the-density-of-creatures))
- **Opinion.** Density stops working when a Commander is revealed and recast repeatedly, in three-player games where everyone watches closely, against the seat beside you in a larger pod, after you have attacked or targeted others, and when a sweeper is likely. ([C1](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01022024-advanced-multiplayer-strategies-2-the-density-of-creatures))
- **Opinion (attack taxes).** Mana taxes on attacking you push opponents to hit other players. Leaks can be blocked with Fog Bank or answered with Swords. The author likes basic-land taxes in decks of three or more colors but gives no amounts. ([C2](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01162024-advanced-multiplayer-theory-4-you-cant-win-until-you-dont-lose))
- **Opinion (earlier pass).** A pillow fort makes attacking its player unappealing and wins through the other players. Sphere of Safety, Ghostly Prison, Windborn Muse and Propaganda are the taxes named; opponents who can pay still attack, and taxing makes attacks on other players more attractive. Punishment effects such as No Mercy are preferred. ([P1](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/jasondookeran-seo-03302026-what-is-mtg-pillow-fort-strategy-breakdown-best-cards))
- **Opinion (earlier pass).** Seal of Doom and Soul Snare make opponents weigh attacks; the 2016 author endorses being seen as dangerous, because hiding strength makes a deck the easier target. ([P4](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/the-problem-with-politics-in-commander/))
- **Example (one list).** The 2025 Pramikon pillowfort list uses Propaganda (a charge per attacker), Windborn Muse, Archangel of Tithes and Champions of Minas Tirith as taxes, and Lightmine Field and Bident of Thassa as punishment for token attackers. Its commander's left-or-right choice limits attacks to one direction. It names Fog Bank, Wall of Vapor and Wall of Denial as blockers and gives no blocker total. ([C15](https://edhrec.com/articles/making-a-pillow-fort-deck-with-pramikon-sky-rampart))
- **Evidence (one list).** That list has 100 cards: 15 creatures, 18 enchantments, 13 artifacts, 9 instants, 4 sorceries, one planeswalker, 39 lands and the commander. It is a pillowfort, not an attacking deck, and the article says a fair amount of its creatures cannot attack. ([C15](https://edhrec.com/articles/making-a-pillow-fort-deck-with-pramikon-sky-rampart))
- **Opinion (hate magnets).** In a 2016 forum thread, Propaganda and Ghostly Prison draw hate mainly for their efficiency. One-sided deterrents (Kazuul) stop attacks only on their controller; whole-table ones (Silent Arbiter, Meekstone) stop attacks on anyone. Crawlspace works against token decks with no drawback when opponents attack each other. One poster says the key is not to interfere with opponents' attacks on each other. ([C7](https://tappedout.net/mtg-forum/commander-deck-help/blockers-and-defences-that-dont-make-you-a-target))
- **Opinion (instant-speed defense).** A 2022 poster advises keeping some creatures home and holding leftover mana for instant-speed responses; another stresses instant-speed protection. ([C5](https://tappedout.net/mtg-forum/commander/dealing-with-politics-as-an-aggro-player))
- **Opinion (protection and recovery).** Teferi's Protection and Unbreakable Formation protect the board; Patriarch's Bidding and Living Death plan recovery. ([C5](https://tappedout.net/mtg-forum/commander/dealing-with-politics-as-an-aggro-player))
- **Anecdote (sweepers).** A 40-creature Naya deck keeps protection, recursion and draw in reserve against sweepers. A friend was left with an empty hand and board after two sweepers. ([C5](https://tappedout.net/mtg-forum/commander/dealing-with-politics-as-an-aggro-player))
- **Opinion (retaliation).** A poster says retaliating against whichever player targets you most makes the table wary. Another says focusing the player least able to absorb damage drew little complaint. ([C5](https://tappedout.net/mtg-forum/commander/dealing-with-politics-as-an-aggro-player))
- **Opinion (politics as defense).** As archenemy, a dominant board and a credible retaliation threat can break up a coalition aimed at you. Breaking a promise carries no rules penalty; the cost is social. At 1 life, players abandon earlier agreements, the author says. ([C12](https://draftsim.com/edh-politics/))
- **Opinion (sweeper planning).** Before an all-in attack, decide how to protect the board from a sweeper. The article calls extra defensive turns a top cause of squandered advantage; this is an author's claim with no evidence. ([P2](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-close-out-commander-games))
- **Opinion (aggro article).** DonSpider's aggro article discusses no defensive creatures; blockers appear only as targets. It names two plans against wipes: indestructible creatures, or recursion and tokens. ([C3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-aggro-edh-deck-for-multiplayer))

### Inferences
- Taxes stop only attackers that cannot pay (P1). The attackers who can pay still attack, and taxes push attacks elsewhere (P1, C2). A tax-based defense still needs its own blockers or vigilance for the biggest attacker. This is my reasoning, not a source's claim; it matches the owner's observation that his attackers were left open.
- The sources describe two defenses that point in different directions: making attacks costly (deathtouch, punishers, rattlesnakes) and making the board look unattackable (density). Conflicts item 1 covers the visibility dispute.
- One-sided deterrents (C7) fit a deck whose table is meant to keep fighting itself. Whole-table deterrents work against the deck's own table position. This rests on one post plus the attack-tax logic.
- C1's list of when density fails includes "after you have attacked": the attack itself changes how the table reads the board. This fits the owner's observation; untested.

### How experts decide what to leave back
No source gives a decision rule with numbers. Two qualitative rules appear: keep flying, reach or deathtouch blockers back ([C2](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01162024-advanced-multiplayer-theory-4-you-cant-win-until-you-dont-lose)), and keep some creatures home with mana open ([C5](https://tappedout.net/mtg-forum/commander/dealing-with-politics-as-an-aggro-player)).

### Gaps
- Defensive piece counts for aggro, typal or Voltron decks: none. C15 gives counts for a pillowfort, not an attacker.
- Crack-back math, and vigilance granted to a team: none beyond one example (C2). Card-level vigilance, such as Aurelia, the Warleader, appears only in an unverified search snippet.
- MTG Wiki's Pillowfort page and Card Kingdom's blockers article were not reachable (HTTP 403).

## 2. Damage through a wide board of chump blockers

### Takeaway
The sources offer evasion that needs two or more blockers (menace), evasion limited by color (fear and intimidate), forced-block cards, and sweepers or attack caps against wide boards. The sources' own data says menace weakens against token decks, and color-based evasion depends on the table's colors. None gives a number of evasive attackers needed.

### Cited findings
- **Evidence (rule).** A menace creature needs at least two blockers. ([C10](https://edhrec.com/articles/evasive-maneuvers-menace))
- **Opinion.** Menace is strong evasion, since a double block can cost a defender two creatures. Most commanders cannot block a menace creature alone, which the author sees as making menace stronger in multiplayer. ([C10](https://edhrec.com/articles/evasive-maneuvers-menace))
- **Opinion.** Against wide boards menace weakens: token and tribal decks add creatures that make double blocks easy, so the author calls menace more dependent on the metagame. ([C10](https://edhrec.com/articles/evasive-maneuvers-menace))
- **Evidence (dated dataset).** EDHREC as of 15 Nov 2020: 171 Commander-legal cards carry or confer menace; token-themed decks were 1.45% of scraped decks (6,176) and tribal decks 17.25% (73,602). ([C10](https://edhrec.com/articles/evasive-maneuvers-menace))
- **Example.** Falthis and Kediss give commander creatures menace and deathtouch, so a little damage kills two blockers. ([C10](https://edhrec.com/articles/evasive-maneuvers-menace))
- **Evidence (rule).** A non-black creature with fear can be blocked only by black or artifact creatures. With intimidate, only creatures sharing its color, or artifacts, can block it. For black creatures the two nearly match. Artifact creatures can block either keyword regardless of color; a colorless creature can be blocked only by artifacts. ([C11](https://edhrec.com/articles/evasive-maneuvers-fear-and-intimidate))
- **Opinion.** Each added color in a creature's color identity makes intimidate less effective, so multicolored creatures suffer most. The author sees intimidate as viable when you know your metagame and go wide. ([C11](https://edhrec.com/articles/evasive-maneuvers-fear-and-intimidate))
- **Evidence (dated dataset).** EDHREC, 2020: the odds that one opponent's commander alone can block an intimidate creature were about 44% for white, 49% for blue and 47% for black, artifacts included. Just under 20% of the top 100 creatures of the previous two years were black. ([C11](https://edhrec.com/articles/evasive-maneuvers-fear-and-intimidate))
- **Opinion (mass removal).** Wide boards are weak to mass removal; Pernicious Deed and Nevinyrral's Disk are the examples. The defenses named are indestructible creatures, self-sacrificing creatures (Hapless Researcher, Sakura-Tribe Elder), and free sacrifice outlets (Viscera Seer, Goblin Bombardment). Do not go wide when the opponents' colors point to sweepers. ([C1](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01022024-advanced-multiplayer-strategies-2-the-density-of-creatures))
- **Opinion (token decks).** Token decks add pump (anthems, Overrun effects) to push combat damage; the article does not cover blocking. Token doublers are weak without tokens in play, so clearing the tokens blanks them. Wrath of God is the named sweeper. Ghostly Prison, Propaganda and Crawlspace are said to hamper profitable attacks. ([C14](https://edhrec.com/articles/deep-analysis-token))
- **Opinion (article's description).** The 2022 "each player" list calls Earthquake the one-sided pick for flier decks, and says Rolling Earthquake can wipe a table late. ([C9](https://edhrec.com/articles/too-specific-top-10-and-each-player))
- **Evidence (rule logic).** A forced block lets excess trample damage through; a double or triple block can cost the defender two creatures. Fight and bite removal wastes damage beyond lethal. ([C4](https://commandersherald.com/?p=37312))
- **Opinion (attack caps).** Crawlspace limits each attack to two creatures per turn. It works against go-wide decks, but a Voltron commander still gets through. Lightmine Field makes each attacker deal more damage as the attack grows. ([P1](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/jasondookeran-seo-03302026-what-is-mtg-pillow-fort-strategy-breakdown-best-cards))
- **Opinion (defender habits).** Players usually block only when forced, fearing for delicate creatures and relying on lifegain. The article generalizes this from the author's games. The 40-life start is a format fact the article states; it means early threats often go unblocked. ([C4](https://commandersherald.com/?p=37312))
- **Opinion.** Mix power and toughness so opponents cannot quickly calculate blocks. Flying and reach blockers stop flyers, and opponents expect mono-green decks to lack them. ([C1](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01022024-advanced-multiplayer-strategies-2-the-density-of-creatures))
- **Anecdote (small attacks).** A 2017 poster's Xenagos deck needs only one or two creatures and ends games suddenly. A 2022 poster keeps tokens small and pumps them for a one-turn kill with Jazal Goldmane, Coat of Arms or Shared Animosity, and avoids casting the pumps early. ([C6](https://tappedout.net/mtg-forum/commander-deck-help/how-to-play-aggro-edh-in-multiplayer); [C5](https://tappedout.net/mtg-forum/commander/dealing-with-politics-as-an-aggro-player))

### Inferences
- Menace is a weak tool against a board built for double blocks, and C10 says so itself. Fear or intimidate can get past a board of one color, but no source gives counts for token colors. By the rule in C11, a black fear attacker cannot be blocked by red tokens. This is untested for the owner's decks.
- The sources' answers to wide boards are sweepers and attack caps (C1, C14, P1), not evasion counts. The owner observed eight or more 1/1 goblins, sacrificed to chump-block and to kill attackers. C1 describes sacrifice outlets as a defense; the sources do not analyze the attacker's side of that exchange.

### Gaps
- Evasive attackers needed per chump blocker: none.
- Mass "can't block" effects, deathtouch or first-strike tokens, and the math of making blocks bad: none found.
- EDHREC color odds and menace counts are from 2020.

## 3. Damage through big blockers

### Takeaway
The sources handle big blockers with forced-block cards, evasion, politics (asking others not to block, or goading) and removal before combat. Nearly every claim is card-level or opinion. No source measures success rates.

### Cited findings
- **Evidence (costs, as described).** Provoke (two mana) untaps the creature you want to block. Lure (three-mana aura) forces blocks. Neyith of the Dire Hunt (three mana) doubles one creature's power and forces it to block, once per combat. Anzrag, the Quake-Mole (seven-mana activation) adds an extra combat step per forced block. Enlarge (+7/+7 and trample) lets damage through the block. ([C4](https://commandersherald.com/?p=37312))
- **Opinion.** Invasion Plans (every creature must block if able), paired with a rampage commander, nearly guarantees a win in the author's view, and he calls it the key enabler for that deck. ([C4](https://commandersherald.com/?p=37312))
- **Evidence (costs).** Disturbed Slumber, Elemental Uprising and Vengeant Earth each animate one of your lands into a 4/4 attacker for the turn to force a block. The risk is losing the land. ([C4](https://commandersherald.com/?p=37312))
- **Evidence (logic).** Predatory Rampage makes the defender's own creatures block. It targets nothing on that side, so the author says it gets past hexproof, shroud and ward. Ward and hexproof make targeted removal less dependable. ([C4](https://commandersherald.com/?p=37312))
- **Opinion.** Taunting Elf and its cousins (Golgari Decoy, Riveteers Decoy) pull blockers away from your real damage dealers. Gaea's Protector clears a path for big attackers. Forced blocks sidestep protection from Lightning Greaves and Swiftfoot Boots. ([C4](https://commandersherald.com/?p=37312))
- **Opinion.** Indrik Umbra gives a boost and first strike, so the creature survives several blockers. ([C4](https://commandersherald.com/?p=37312))
- **Opinion (politics).** To push an attacker through, ask others not to block it, sweeten the request, and point to another player as the bigger threat. Goading a rival's big creature makes it attack someone else. When a big creature makes you the focus and you lack a quick finish, trade it for a weaker one and keep a low profile. ([C12](https://draftsim.com/edh-politics/))
- **Evidence (rule logic, fan format).** Goad makes the goaded creature attack a player other than the goader when able, and attack taxes are checked before goad. The post is about Tiny Leaders: Reborn, not Commander; check the Comprehensive Rules before use. ([C8](https://tiny-leaders-reborn.blogspot.com/2025/10/goad-and-combat-taxes-in-multiplayer.html))
- **Opinion (earlier pass).** Goad's weak spot is haste ([P1](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/jasondookeran-seo-03302026-what-is-mtg-pillow-fort-strategy-breakdown-best-cards)). Quality beats quantity: five 2/2s can be outclassed by a few high-impact permanents, so spend removal on those first ([P3](https://draftsim.com/mtg-commander-threat-assessment/)).
- **Opinion (target choice).** Hit the player whose cheap commander lets them field defenses sooner, while they are still exposed. If a player would kill your attacker anyway, get damage in first. ([C13](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/who-should-i-attack/))
- **Anecdote.** A 2022 poster: 1 to 3 damage is fine with a clear reason; 10 damage turns that player against you; a finishing hit is worth more. ([C5](https://tappedout.net/mtg-forum/commander/dealing-with-politics-as-an-aggro-player))

### Inferences
- Forced-block cards are the nearest tools to making big blockers block badly. They are card-level, come from one article, and are untested in pods.
- The owner's losses (fragile attackers into big blockers) match P3's advice and C13's, but no source measures how often either works.

### Gaps
- How often a big blocker stops an attack, or how many removal or forced-block cards a deck needs: none.
- The card descriptions in C4 were not checked against card text.

## 4. Closing against three opponents

### Takeaway
A single-target kill takes one resolution per opponent; team-wide damage, drains and overruns can end several opponents at once. The sources call the second group faster, but none measures reliability or speed with three opponents. Their route counts are opinions (2 to 3 routes; one main plan plus one fallback in an unverified snippet). Owner observation: his single-target kill is slow against three players.

### Cited findings
- **Evidence (format arithmetic).** Three opponents at 40 life each total 120, against 20 in a one-on-one game. ([C3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-aggro-edh-deck-for-multiplayer)) C11 frames the 120 total as making combat tricky; that is opinion.
- **Opinion.** Aggro is harder in Commander because early rushes are less likely as player count and total life rise. ([C3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-aggro-edh-deck-for-multiplayer)) A 2017 poster says the opposite for a table that races: 120 total life is manageable. ([C6](https://tappedout.net/mtg-forum/commander-deck-help/how-to-play-aggro-edh-in-multiplayer))
- **Evidence (card, as described).** The 2021 token analysis lists Overrun effects (Craterhoof Behemoth, Return of the Wildspeaker, Overrun, Overwhelming Stampede, Beastmaster Ascension) and non-combat pings (Impact Tremors, Goblin Bombardment, Purphoros) that win without attacking. ([C14](https://edhrec.com/articles/deep-analysis-token))
- **Opinion (each-player effects, 2022 list).** Hurricane (everyone loses X life for X mana; the article says to use it when you lead on life). Pestilence Demon (an eight-mana flier that clears creatures and damages each player every turn while mana lasts; the author says it ends games fast). Chandra's Ignition (a red finisher hitting each opponent). Volcanic Fallout (each player loses two life). Crypt Rats (can finish low-life players). Pestilence and Pyrohemia (repeatable). Arcbond. Archangel Avacyn's flip sweeps much of the battlefield. At 40 life, the author plans repeated wipes. ([C9](https://edhrec.com/articles/too-specific-top-10-and-each-player))
- **Evidence (note).** Symmetric effects hit you too; the Hurricane entry depends on your lead on life. ([C9](https://edhrec.com/articles/too-specific-top-10-and-each-player))
- **Opinion (aggro article).** Jetmir, an anthem-based damage converter, is ranked nearly with Craterhoof Behemoth as a finisher. Edric and Commissar Severina Raine are named as closers without detail. The author says a win should come from combat damage rather than a combo, ideally by turn 7 or 8 (timing unsourced). In four-player games, pressure splits the table's focus. ([C3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-aggro-edh-deck-for-multiplayer))
- **Taxonomy (earlier pass).** P2 lists four finishing types: combat, combo, drain and overrun. ([P2](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-close-out-commander-games))
- **Example (earlier pass).** An overrun that kills two opponents and leaves the third at 4 life counts as a win next turn. ([P2](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-close-out-commander-games))
- **Opinion (earlier pass, no evidence).** Each deck has two to three finishing routes. With three opponents, the table gets about three answers per card you draw. ([P2](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-close-out-commander-games))
- **Unverified (earlier pass).** Playgroup reports, from about 645,000 tracked games, that about half of decided multiplayer wins come from combat, about a fifth from non-combat effects such as drain, and about 8% each from combo and commander damage. Method and date not seen. ([P6](https://playgroup.gg/commander/how-to-play))
- **Unverified (snippet).** One main finishing plan with one credible fallback; extra card draw alone does not finish a game. ([mtgapp](https://mtgapp.com/?p=140))
- **Anecdote.** A 2022 poster: a 40-damage finishing hit beats 10 damage that turns the table against you. A 2017 poster gangs up on one opponent until they are out, and admits it is mean. ([C5](https://tappedout.net/mtg-forum/commander/dealing-with-politics-as-an-aggro-player); [C6](https://tappedout.net/mtg-forum/commander-deck-help/how-to-play-aggro-edh-in-multiplayer))
- **Anecdote.** A 2017 poster's elf aggro deck gets about 80% of its wins from a combo, and they usually avoid aggro against combo decks. ([C6](https://tappedout.net/mtg-forum/commander-deck-help/how-to-play-aggro-edh-in-multiplayer))
- **Opinion (go wide).** Go-wide decks want low opposing totals. A go-wide deck can likely take out three players near 20 life (an example). ([P3](https://draftsim.com/mtg-commander-threat-assessment/))
- **Opinion (threat order).** An overpowered commander should expect to be targeted. Pressure a clear leader before they have attacked you. ([C13](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/who-should-i-attack/))

### Inferences
- A single-target kill needs one resolution per opponent, so in a four-player pod it needs three where a team-wide effect needs one. This is arithmetic, not a measured speed difference.
- The sources point to a single-target kill as a backup, with a team-wide or damage route as the main plan (P2's overrun example, C3's converter role, C14's non-combat pings). Untested.
- Symmetric effects (C9) help only if the controller's own board and life survive them. The sources do not say how often that holds in pods.

### Gaps
- Reliability or speed of single-target versus team-wide finishers with three opponents: none.
- Extra combats: only Aurelia (C3) and Anzrag (C4); no count.
- Library of Leng's win-conditions article (part 2) was cut off; not usable.

## 5. Haste, speed, and protecting attackers

### Takeaway
Haste enablers appear in the sources only in passing (one creature in the 2026 aggro article) and in unverified snippets. No source counts how many a deck needs. Protection is discussed as wipe insurance and board recovery, not as protection of attackers against blocks or sacrifice.

### Cited findings
- **Opinion.** DonSpider names one haste creature, Aurelia, the Warleader, which the author rates very highly. The article describes her as having haste and allowing extra combat phases. ([C3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-aggro-edh-deck-for-multiplayer))
- **Opinion.** Aggro needs speed, durability and payoffs, and not every standard aggro card works in Commander. Cheap creatures alone do not do it. ([C3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-aggro-edh-deck-for-multiplayer))
- **Example (arithmetic).** With ten ramp pieces, four-drops come down on turn 3 and six-drops on turn 4. ([C3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-aggro-edh-deck-for-multiplayer))
- **Opinion (unsourced).** The article says 10 to 12 ramp sources is the standard for mid-power decks, aggro should aim near 10 and put ramp ahead of early card draw, and low-ramp aggro needs an average of 2.8 or lower to keep pace. ([C3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-aggro-edh-deck-for-multiplayer))
- **Example and opinion.** Playing only one- and two-drops leads to missed plays by turn 5. Skipping two-drops leaves three turns with nothing to do. The author calls a curve error the most common Commander building mistake; the wording does not say which one. ([C3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-aggro-edh-deck-for-multiplayer))
- **Opinion.** Ohran Frostfang gives every attacker deathtouch, so blocking costs the defender. Boros Charm (two mana) gives double strike or board indestructibility; the author calls it a must-include. Teferi's Protection, Make a Stand and Selfless Spirit are wipe insurance. After a wrath, keep equipment and play big threats so one or two creatures pressure. ([C3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-aggro-edh-deck-for-multiplayer))
- **Opinion.** Two plans against wipes: indestructible creatures, or recursion and token production. ([C3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-aggro-edh-deck-for-multiplayer))
- **Opinion (2017 poster).** A serious table leaves two options for an attacker: combat combos (Sword of Feast and Famine with Aggravated Assault) or straight Voltron (Sigarda, Uril and Bruna are named). No one explains how these shield attackers. ([C6](https://tappedout.net/mtg-forum/commander-deck-help/how-to-play-aggro-edh-in-multiplayer))
- **Opinion (mass removal defenses).** Indestructible creatures and free sacrifice outlets are the defenses named against mass removal. ([C1](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01022024-advanced-multiplayer-strategies-2-the-density-of-creatures))
- **Unverified (snippets).** Gamertag Mythras splits team haste (Anger, Fervor, Rhythm of the Wild, Fires of Yavimaya, Urabrask) from single-target haste with protection for one or two key creatures. Crashing Drawbridge is a low-cost haste source that tends to lose at higher-power tables. Accelerate is a haste cantrip. ([U1](https://gamertagmythras.com/blog/magic-the-gathering/best-mtg-haste-enablers); [U3](https://casualplaneswalker.com/?p=51222); [U4](https://moxmythic.com/cards/accelerate))
- **Unverified (snippet).** The Geekydomain Voltron guide ranks built-in protection first among the commander traits it values, and notes that a gear-based commander can be recast from the command zone. ([U5](https://geekydomain.com/blogs/guides/voltron-building-around-one-big-threat))
- **Evidence (official, earlier pass).** Bracket 3 expects at least six turns before a game ends. The figure marks when an ending should leave players satisfied, not a hard limit. ([P5](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/announcements/commander-brackets-beta-update-october-21-2025))

### Inferences
- The snippets suggest two haste types: team haste for wide boards and single-target haste for one or two key creatures. If accurate, a deck with fragile attackers and no haste needs them to survive a turn before attacking. No source quantifies that.
- Protection in the sources is against wipes and targeted removal (ward, hexproof, Lightning Greaves). None addresses blocks or sacrifice, which is the owner's problem.

### Gaps
- Haste enablers and protection cards per combat deck: none.
- Protection of attackers against multi-blocks and sacrifice: none.
- Snippet claims (U1 to U5) are unverified; fetching those pages is the next step.

## 6. Common mistakes of aggro and typal decks in multiplayer, and the fixes

### Takeaway
The recurring mistakes: attacking the player who will become the table's target with no defense behind, attacking early and drawing fire, a curve with too few early plays, going wide into a likely sweeper, and misreading what the table plays. The fixes are qualitative. The only numbers are opinions (ramp, curve, turns).

### Cited findings
- **Anecdote.** A 2017 poster's Aurelia aggro deck worked but made them the table's main target. Their only answer was to win before the table finished them. ([C6](https://tappedout.net/mtg-forum/commander-deck-help/how-to-play-aggro-edh-in-multiplayer))
- **Anecdote and opinion.** A 2022 poster: chip away early instead of hitting one player hard; keep tokens small until a one-turn pump finishes; avoid casting the pumps early. ([C5](https://tappedout.net/mtg-forum/commander/dealing-with-politics-as-an-aggro-player))
- **Opinion.** Casual players skip careful math and pick the easiest-looking attack. The density advice is written against that habit. ([C1](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01022024-advanced-multiplayer-strategies-2-the-density-of-creatures))
- **Opinion.** Stop piling onto a player once their threat is answered; hold removal unless timing is critical. ([P3](https://draftsim.com/mtg-commander-threat-assessment/))
- **Opinion (earlier pass).** Check whether the deck can beat any one opponent one-on-one, and note what it struggles to interact with. ([P4](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/the-problem-with-politics-in-commander/))
- **Opinion (curve).** The curve errors in C3: a deck of only one- and two-drops misses plays by turn 5, and skipping two-drops leaves three turns with nothing to do. ([C3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-aggro-edh-deck-for-multiplayer))
- **Opinion (aggro in Commander).** Aggro needs its own approach rather than Standard or Modern aggro cards. ([C3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-aggro-edh-deck-for-multiplayer))
- **Opinion (sweepers).** Avoid density when a sweeper is likely, and do not go wide when the opponents' colors or archetype point to sweepers. ([C1](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/abesargent-01022024-advanced-multiplayer-strategies-2-the-density-of-creatures))
- **Opinion (earlier pass).** Decide how to protect the board before an all-in attack. ([P2](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-close-out-commander-games))
- **Not used as evidence.** A 2022 forum post gives a 25% average aggro win ratio with no source; it is listed as unverified under Numbers at a glance.

### Inferences
- The fix the sources share is to keep the defensive board and the attack count in balance before going all-in. This matches the owner's observation, but no source measures it.

### Gaps
- No fetched source tests any fix. The set is opinion and anecdote, dated 2016 to 2026.
- Card Kingdom's archetype and mistakes articles were not read: the domain returned 403 for the blockers article, and the others were not attempted within budget.

## Conflicts between sources

1. **Visibility.** P4 (2016) and C1 (2024) say looking dangerous, or having many bodies, deters attacks. P3 (2025) advises talking down your own threat so an opponent spends removal on it. The first wants attacks deterred; the second wants answers spent. Different aims, opposite signals.
2. **Whom to attack.** P4: from a strong position, remove the weaker players first. P3: dealing with the leader can hand them the game, so ally with the trailing player against the leader. C13 (2017): pressure a clear leader before they attack. C5 (2022, posted): hit control players first, and another poster focuses the player least able to absorb damage. No consensus.
3. **Spread or focus.** C5 (2022): chip away and do not hit one player hard. C6 (2017): gang up on one opponent until they are out (anecdote). Direct conflict.
4. **Hold back or race.** C5 (2022): keep creatures home. C6 (2017): no real fix for being targeted; race to win first. Opposite defenses.
5. **Is 120 total life good for aggro?** C6 (2017) says it is manageable for a racing table. C3 (2026) says aggro is harder because early rushes are less likely. C11 (2020) says the 120 framing makes combat tricky. Opinion against opinion.
6. **Taxes as defense.** P1 (2026) says taxes are not the best defense and prefers punishment effects such as No Mercy. C2 (2024) likes basic-land taxes in decks of three or more colors and rates No Mercy as the top rattlesnake. Both say taxes push attacks onto other players. A difference of preference, not of effect.
7. **Menace as strength.** C10 calls menace strong evasion and also says it weakens against wide token boards. Read it as: strong against boards with few creatures, weak against wide ones.
8. **Three-player aggro.** A TappedOut thread (search snippet, unverified) says aggro performs best at three players. C3 and P2 say more players make aggro harder or split pressure. Unverified against opinion.
9. **Turn targets.** C3 gives turn 7 or 8 as an aggro target; P5 gives six turns as the Bracket 3 expectation. Different measures (an opinion target and an official expectation), not a contradiction.
10. **Deterrents and the table.** C7 says one-sided deterrents keep the table fighting, while whole-table deterrents work against everyone. C1 and C2 do not discuss this split. Single source.
