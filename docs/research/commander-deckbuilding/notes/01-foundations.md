## Key question 1: what process do expert builders follow?

### Takeaway
The only ordered build process found is the community "8x8 method", which reserves 35 land slots first and then fills eight themes of eight cards each. No fetched source states the brief's order (game plan, win conditions, packages, then mana and interaction) in an expert's own words.

### Cited findings
- The 8x8 method is a "popular community framework", not an official rule. Steps: count the commander as card one; reserve 35 unfilled land slots; choose eight themes that fit the deck's goals; fill each theme with eight cards; check 35 + 64 + 1 = 100 ([S2](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/deck-building-basics-ratios-curves-the-8x8-method)).
- Example themes: ramp, card draw, removal, board wipes plus protection (one theme), and four strategy packages (examples: token generation, anthem boosts, sacrifice rewards, finishers), eight cards each ([S2](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/deck-building-basics-ratios-curves-the-8x8-method)).
- The page calls its baseline "training wheels, not scripture" and says the categories are flexible ([S2](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/deck-building-basics-ratios-curves-the-8x8-method)).
- The Star City Games article has no deck-building sequence and no composition numbers. Its four sample builds start from the commander's core plan and add backups; that order is the summariser's reading, not stated advice ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/magic-the-gathering/select/how-to-win-every-game-of-commander-you-play-in-2022-and-beyond/)).
- Redundancy example: if the commander is shut down, two other creatures still let the same engine produce unlimited mana for creature spells ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/magic-the-gathering/select/how-to-win-every-game-of-commander-you-play-in-2022-and-beyond/)).
- The article suggests choosing a power identity before building; its sample decks differ in how high-powered they are ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/magic-the-gathering/select/how-to-win-every-game-of-commander-you-play-in-2022-and-beyond/)).

### Inferences
- The 8x8 fixes the mana base (35 lands) before the plan is chosen. That is the reverse of the brief's implied order, so these sources do not settle the sequence.
- Four strategy packages of eight cards give 32 slots to plans. Read against the owner's consistency rule (one declared plan with enough pieces of each function), the template fits only if those packages are alternatives, not stacked. This is my reading, not a source claim.

### Gaps
- No expert source found for "game plan, then win conditions, then packages, then base".
- Not searched within budget: Sheldon Menery's columns, ChannelFireball, Commander's Herald, Hipsters of the Coast, TCGplayer, MTGGoldfish, Wargamer, Polygon, Cardmarket Insight. Card Kingdom returned HTTP 403 ([F2](https://blog.cardkingdom.com/?p=30299)).
- The 8x8's origin was not traced. Other sites' titles repeat the name in search results; not opened.

## Key question 2: the templates, and how experts adjust them by strategy

### Takeaway
The only concrete template in the fetched text is the 8x8 (35 lands; eight themes of eight cards). The Command Zone template and its 2025 "new era" revision could not be read: the episode page returned HTTP 403 and no show notes were found. No fetched source gives strategy-specific ratios for Commander.

### Cited findings
- The Command Zone episode 658 is titled "Commander Deckbuilding Template for the New Era". The title appears in a search result's URL; the page returned HTTP 403 ([F1](https://ppacc.player.fm/series/the-command-zone-1927096/commander-deckbuilding-template-for-the-new-era-658)). Its numbers are not retrieved.
- Search results also list episode 659 ("The Problem with Deckbuilding Templates") and an older episode 379 for the template. These are EDHREC titles, not opened; see Gaps. A search summary ties 379 to the template; unverified.
- Hosts, per a podcast-directory snippet: Rachel Weeks, Jimmy Wong, Josh Lee Kwai ([S12](https://podcastrex.com/shows/the-command-zone)).
- 8x8 page, beginner section (as summarised): 36–38 lands and 10–12 draw sources, against the 8x8 default of 35 lands and 8 draw cards. Many builders run ramp and draw slightly above eight, taking the extra slots from a strategy package ([S2](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/deck-building-basics-ratios-curves-the-8x8-method)).
- The Casual Planeswalker FAQ says, for Commander, to align the creature count with your approach; it gives no number ([S3](https://casualplaneswalker.com/faq/deckbuilding/)).
- 60-card archetype creature ranges, complementary and not Commander: aggro 25–30, midrange 15–22, control 4–10, fewer when planeswalkers close games ([S3](https://casualplaneswalker.com/faq/deckbuilding/)).
- Strategy examples, not ratios: a token deck should find room for a token maker; an artifact deck suits an artifact payoff; mono-green decks value an artifact answer because green has little removal; decks with modal double-faced lands benefit from bounce ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/magic-the-gathering/select/how-to-win-every-game-of-commander-you-play-in-2022-and-beyond/)).

### Inferences
- The owner's observations (ramp for landfall, draw for instants) fit the 8x8 rule of raising one theme above eight at the cost of a strategy package. Neither source sizes the change. Unverified.
- The 8x8 has no creature or evasion slot; its nearest categories are board wipes with protection and finishers. A combat deck built on it must get its creatures and evasion from the strategy packages. This bears on owner lesson 2; no source tests it.

### Gaps
- Command Zone template numbers and the 2025 revision: missing. Transcript sites index other Command Zone episodes in search results; episode 658 is not confirmed on any of them.
- No ratio table for aggro, voltron, landfall, spellslinger, combo, control or tokens in Commander. The search "Commander deck ratios by archetype" returned only stats-site index pages and a test-subdomain page with 60-card counts; both were discarded.

## Key question 3: how the commander shapes the deck

### Takeaway
The sources treat the commander as the centre of the plan: its synergies become the main plan, costly commanders need card flow and recursion, and the deck needs backups for the commander's engine. No fetched source covers commander tax, repeated commander removal, or a commander-centric versus commander-independent split.

### Cited findings
- The commander's synergy becomes the central plan, which opponents tend to target; the sample builds run on commander-specific engines ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/magic-the-gathering/select/how-to-win-every-game-of-commander-you-play-in-2022-and-beyond/)).
- High-cost commanders need support: card flow, life-for-mana, and recasting from the command zone ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/magic-the-gathering/select/how-to-win-every-game-of-commander-you-play-in-2022-and-beyond/)).
- A costly commander may be cast rarely or never, so the other cards must carry the games ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/magic-the-gathering/select/how-to-win-every-game-of-commander-you-play-in-2022-and-beyond/)).
- Backups for the engine are the defence if the commander is removed ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/magic-the-gathering/select/how-to-win-every-game-of-commander-you-play-in-2022-and-beyond/)).
- A card can be picked because it works with the commander: Kosin's Wanderer's Twig interacts with Jhoira, Weatherlight Captain ([S6](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/davekosin-10232020-no-sweat-my-pet-cards)).

### Inferences
- A commander can be removed, so a single-commander engine with no backup is fragile. S1 implies this; no source quantifies how often removal happens.
- The 8x8 counts the commander only as card one and names no commander category; a commander plan would have to sit inside the strategy packages. My reading.

### Gaps
- Commander tax: not found in the fetched text; not searched.
- Repeated removal of the commander, and the commander-centric versus commander-independent split: not found.

## Key question 4: mana curve and card-type mix

### Takeaway
Two Commander figures are sourced: 25–35 creatures per 100 cards and a 35–38 land starting range ([S3](https://casualplaneswalker.com/faq/deckbuilding/)). The sources disagree on the curve shape. No source gives average mana value targets, plays by turn 2 or 3, or noncreature engine counts.

### Cited findings
- Commander: 25–35 creatures in a typical deck; start with 35–38 lands; curves usually peak around 3–4 mana; singleton, one copy of each card ([S3](https://casualplaneswalker.com/faq/deckbuilding/)).
- The same FAQ's 60-card reference points: 22–26 lands; competitive curve peak at 2–3 mana. Labelled 60-card ([S3](https://casualplaneswalker.com/faq/deckbuilding/)).
- 8x8 page: 35 lands and no creature guidance. Beginner section: 36–38 lands. The curve should be densest at two and three mana and thin above; each card costing five or more should justify itself; a steeper curve in four-player pods works only if ramp rises to match ([S2](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/deck-building-basics-ratios-curves-the-8x8-method)).
- Snippet only: a guide estimates about 50 mana sources, ramp included, for a deck averaging 3.5 mana value ([S8](https://geekydomain.com/blogs/guides/mtg-commander-mana-curve-basics)). Unverified.
- Snippet only: a TCG Protectors guide uses 37–38 land slots and calls its structure a flexible guideline ([S9](https://tcgprotectors.com/blogs/magic-the-gathering-blog/mtg-commander-deck-building-guide)). Unverified.

### Inferences
- If the 50-source snippet is right, it implies 12–15 ramp pieces on top of 35–38 lands. That is arithmetic on an unverified figure; check before use.

### Gaps
- Average mana value targets; plays by turn 2 or 3; creature counts for creature-based plans beyond the 25–35 range; noncreature engine, removal and draw counts. The FAQ gives no ramp, removal or draw count for Commander.

## Key question 5: Wizards' Commander Brackets

### Takeaway
Bracket 3 ("Upgraded") allows at most three Game Changers. Draftsim (secondary) says it expects games of about six or more turns, that two-card combos cannot come online before turn six, and that it bars wholesale land destruction and chained extra turns. Wizards' page gives the bracket names and the Game Changers caps, not the per-bracket expectations or the list.

### Cited findings
- Wizards: five brackets, Exhibition (1), Core (2), Upgraded (3), Optimized (4) and cEDH (5). The page calls the system "entirely optional" and "currently in beta" ([S4](https://magic.wizards.com/en/content/commander-format)).
- Wizards: Game Changers caps: Brackets 1 and 2 none; Bracket 3 up to three; Brackets 4 and 5 no limit ([S4](https://magic.wizards.com/en/content/commander-format)).
- Wizards: names "two-card infinite combos", "extra turns" and "mass land denial" as benchmarks and refers to a list it does not include. It gives no version date (footer 1993–2026 only). The fetched text had no Game Changers card names ([S4](https://magic.wizards.com/en/content/commander-format)).
- Expected game length (Draftsim): Bracket 1 nine or more turns; Bracket 2 eight; Bracket 3 six; Bracket 4 four; Bracket 5 no minimum ([S5](https://draftsim.com/mtg-commander-power-bracket/)).
- Per-bracket limits (Draftsim): Brackets 1 and 2: no two-card infinite combos and no wholesale land destruction; Bracket 1 no extra-turn spells; Bracket 2 small amounts of extra turns, never chained. Bracket 3: at most three Game Changers; two-card combos cannot come online before turn six; infinite combos allowed as late-game lines; no wholesale land destruction; no chained extra turns. Bracket 4: no limit beyond the ban list; more than three Game Changers, or wholesale land destruction, or excessive extra turns usually places a deck here. Bracket 5: only the ban list ([S5](https://draftsim.com/mtg-commander-power-bracket/)).
- Tutors: no separate rule; five tutors are on the Game Changers list (Demonic, Enlightened, Mystical, Vampiric, Worldly Tutor) and count toward the Bracket 3 cap ([S5](https://draftsim.com/mtg-commander-power-bracket/)).
- Game Changers count: 53 entries (10 creatures, 1 planeswalker, 12 instants, 8 sorceries, 7 enchantments, 8 artifacts, 7 lands) ([S5](https://draftsim.com/mtg-commander-power-bracket/)). Other guides in the search results also give 53 after a 9 February 2026 revision; not opened ([S11](https://scrollvault.net/guides/game-changers.html)).
- List changes: cards are added when linked to problematic or high-powered play, not raw power alone; unbanned cards start on the list; Biorhythm and Coalition Victory moved there from the ban list; Sway of the Stars came off a list for its high cost; no fixed update schedule ([S5](https://draftsim.com/mtg-commander-power-bracket/)).
- Building advice: estimate the realistic winning turn; take the lowest bracket whose criteria all fit; check every card, lands included, against the full Game Changers list; one Game Changer makes a deck Bracket 3 at minimum; discuss expectations before the first game. Precons sit around Bracket 2 ([S5](https://draftsim.com/mtg-commander-power-bracket/)).
- Sol Ring is outside the system; Draftsim expects it in roughly 99% of decks, as an estimate ([S5](https://draftsim.com/mtg-commander-power-bracket/)).
- A Scrollvault snippet also bars two-card infinite combos in Brackets 1–2 and before turn six in Bracket 3 ([S10](https://scrollvault.net/guides/commander-brackets.html)); consistent with S5.
- Conflict inside S5: a reply in its comments says one Ancient Tomb would make a precon Bracket 4; the article body does not say so. The body is followed here ([S5](https://draftsim.com/mtg-commander-power-bracket/)).

### Inferences
- Lands count against the Bracket 3 gate: Draftsim lists lands on the Game Changers list (its examples are Ancient Tomb and Gaea's Cradle), so the land base can break the cap as well as the spells.
- Draftsim expects that Dockside Extortionist, Iona and Prophet of Kruphix could join the list. That is its expectation, not a change.

### Gaps
- Wizards' full February 2025 announcement and the official Game Changers list: not read. The search returned an MTGO news page and an ICv2 item (titles only).
- The October 2025 Brackets update: an EDHREC title in search results; not opened.
- Whether Draftsim's turn counts match Wizards' full text: unverified.

## Key question 6: what separates strong decks, according to experienced authors

### Takeaway
The authors read here disagree. Bennie Smith champions pet cards and "mini-quests", short combos built for fun. Dave Kosin defends personal picks and says a card's value depends on the game stage. A 2023 snippet says competitive builders avoid pet cards. No fetched source supports the brief's rule that every card must do something on its own; that rule is the owner's, not an expert consensus found here.

### Cited findings
- Mini-quests: card pairings that do something short of a win and are fun to run; some link to the commander, others are for entertainment or rules quirks ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/magic-the-gathering/select/how-to-win-every-game-of-commander-you-play-in-2022-and-beyond/)).
- Pet cards: pack in as many as possible; good candidates amuse, look odd or are rarely played ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/magic-the-gathering/select/how-to-win-every-game-of-commander-you-play-in-2022-and-beyond/)).
- Unsourced claim in S1: 75% of players end up unhappy when four players all aim to win. Unverified.
- Kosin: pet cards are legitimate personal choices; no ratio or checklist is given; judge a card by game stage, deck and the wider pool. Example: an early ramp card excels on turn two and is weak on turn seven without a draw engine ([S6](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/davekosin-10232020-no-sweat-my-pet-cards)).
- Kosin: players should not police each other's picks ([S6](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/davekosin-10232020-no-sweat-my-pet-cards)).
- Snippet: Stephen Johnson's 2023 Cool Stuff article treats pet cards and suboptimal picks as things builders generally avoid ([S7](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/stephenjohnson-03202023-cards-to-consider-for-competitive-commander)).
- Search summary only (page returned HTTP 403): Card Kingdom's blog encourages unexpected pet cards over popular staples to avoid predictable lists ([F2](https://blog.cardkingdom.com/?p=30299)). Unverified.

### Inferences
- Kosin's point about game stage is close to owner lesson 4 (a card whose value needs a missing mechanic is a trap), but Kosin does not say that.
- S1's mini-quests run against the owner's rule that mini-combos must not be dead alone. The project must choose its criterion; these sources do not decide it.

### Gaps
- No fetched source on consistency metrics (land variance, redundancy counts), on focus versus several plans, or on owner lesson 5 (score-driven builders cutting role players).
- Not searched within budget: Sheldon Menery's columns, Hipsters of the Coast, Commander's Herald, ChannelFireball, TCGplayer, MTGGoldfish, Cardmarket Insight, Wargamer, Polygon.

## Conflicts between sources

1. **Land count.** 35 in the 8x8 walkthrough ([S2](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/deck-building-basics-ratios-curves-the-8x8-method)); 36–38 in its beginner section ([S2](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/deck-building-basics-ratios-curves-the-8x8-method)); 35–38 ([S3](https://casualplaneswalker.com/faq/deckbuilding/)); 37–38 in a snippet ([S9](https://tcgprotectors.com/blogs/magic-the-gathering-blog/mtg-commander-deck-building-guide)). Use 35–38 and name the source of each figure.
2. **Draw sources.** 8 as the 8x8 default ([S2](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/deck-building-basics-ratios-curves-the-8x8-method)) against 10–12 in its beginner section (same page).
3. **Curve shape.** Common Commander curves peak at 3–4 ([S3](https://casualplaneswalker.com/faq/deckbuilding/)); the bulk should sit at 2–3 ([S2](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/deck-building-basics-ratios-curves-the-8x8-method)). Descriptive against prescriptive; unresolved.
4. **Pet cards and mini-combos.** Pack them in and enjoy mini-quests ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/magic-the-gathering/select/how-to-win-every-game-of-commander-you-play-in-2022-and-beyond/)); defend personal picks ([S6](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/davekosin-10232020-no-sweat-my-pet-cards)); builders generally avoid them (snippet, [S7](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/stephenjohnson-03202023-cards-to-consider-for-competitive-commander)). The owner's brief rejects mini-combos that are dead alone. Three positions, no consensus.
5. **Bracket detail.** The Draftsim body ([S5](https://draftsim.com/mtg-commander-power-bracket/)) against its comment reply (one Ancient Tomb makes a precon Bracket 4). The body is used.
6. **Sequence.** The 8x8 fixes lands before the plan ([S2](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/deck-building-basics-ratios-curves-the-8x8-method)); the brief's implied order puts the plan first. Unresolved.
7. **Template against owner rule (design tension, not a source disagreement).** The 8x8 gives four strategy packages of eight cards each; the owner's consistency rule requires one declared plan.
