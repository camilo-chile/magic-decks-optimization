## 1. Landfall and lands-matter

### Takeaway

No fetched source counts landfall itself. The sources give a general ramp total (8 to 10 ramp spells on a 40-land base, S2) and the landfall mechanics: extra land drops, several lands per spell, land sacrifice that still nets a land, and rare repeated land entries (S1). Land recursion, landfall payoffs and lands-matter win lines are absent from every fetched source.

### Cited findings

- General total: 8 to 10 ramp spells for a first Commander deck on a 40-land base. Ramp never replaces lands. Decks that want the commander out early or cast many X spells probably need more; decks of mostly 1- and 2-mana spells may need none ([S2](http://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/markwischkaemper-06222023-how-much-ramp-is-right-for-your-commander-deck)).
- Baselines differ. S1's sample deck has 37 lands and 16 artifacts, a sample and not a rule ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/commander-fundamentals-ramp/)). S3 says Commander decks typically run 38 to 40 lands, without a source of its own ([S3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-edh-reanimator-deck)). S2, citing Frank Karsten as summarized in its text, gives 42 lands plus Sol Ring at 2 mana and 38 lands plus Sol Ring and 9 other rocks at 6 mana ([S2](http://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/markwischkaemper-06222023-how-much-ramp-is-right-for-your-commander-deck)).
- Definitions differ. S1 counts only spells that put lands onto the battlefield and excludes mana rocks ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/commander-fundamentals-ramp/)). S2 counts land fetch, rocks and mana creatures, ranks them in that order, and excludes spells that only put lands into hand ([S2](http://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/markwischkaemper-06222023-how-much-ramp-is-right-for-your-commander-deck)).
- Turn value: one land per turn is the normal limit; ramp gains value once lands outnumber turns; past about five mana a spell is no longer really ramp ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/commander-fundamentals-ramp/)).
- Extra land drops: Oracle of Mul Daya gives one more land a turn; S1 advises playing the top card of the library first, since another land may lie beneath it. Extra land plays need a land already in hand. Storm Cauldron is on the additional-land list, but S1 says it will not really ramp ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/commander-fundamentals-ramp/)).
- Several lands per spell: Hour of Promise (two lands of any type, including two fetchlands), Far Wanderings (three instead of one, with threshold), Deep Reconnaissance (two lands at a steep cost) ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/commander-fundamentals-ramp/)).
- Land sacrifice that nets a land: Harrow and Krosan Verge sacrifice one land and fetch two; S1 counts them as ramp ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/commander-fundamentals-ramp/)).
- Repeated entries: Farhaven Elf is the only explicit case; when creatures are bounced or cast from the graveyard, its land search can happen again. S1 calls Dreamscape Artist strong in reanimator decks but does not explain repeated entries for it ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/commander-fundamentals-ramp/)).
- Fetch lands: Farseek, Nature's Lore and Ranger's Path find dual lands ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/commander-fundamentals-ramp/)). Land recursion is not in the article; its nearest case, Sun Titan regrowing Burnished Hart, returns a creature ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/commander-fundamentals-ramp/)).
- Non-spell ramp: creatures with enter-the-battlefield triggers (Wood Elves, Solemn Simulacrum), a creature with a death trigger (Viridian Emissary), and abilities on permanents (Sakura-Tribe Elder, Font of Fertility) ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/commander-fundamentals-ramp/)).
- Surplus: ramp that later becomes a card or refills the hand (Commander's Sphere, Bonder's Ornament) keeps its value. If the mana is not needed, Sol Ring is close to dead after the opening hand, so S2 argues against auto-including it. Heavy card draw may justify fewer ramp spells, not fewer lands, unless the deck plays 8-drops ([S2](http://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/markwischkaemper-06222023-how-much-ramp-is-right-for-your-commander-deck)).
- Pods: S1 says Collective Voyage's tapped lands leave the caster to use them last, with New Frontiers sharing the drawback. Ghirapur Orrery is the author's top group-hug pick because everyone gains from it ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/commander-fundamentals-ramp/)).

### Inferences

- **Inference:** landfall needs two counts the sources keep apart: mana sources (the S2 total or more) and land drops (S1's cards). Count extra land drops on their own line, not inside the 8 to 10.
- **Inference:** S1 says ramp gains value once lands outnumber turns. Extra land drops create that surplus, so late landfall ramp needs a use for the extra mana (S2's condition).
- **Owner signal:** his landfall games needed a very large ramp count and ways to make lands enter repeatedly, ideally several per turn. No source sets a per-turn target. He reports that The Earth King and Seismic Tutelage won landfall games and that a score-driven builder cut both; their card texts were not checked here.

### Gaps

- Landfall payoff counts, land recursion counts and lands-matter win lines: no fetched source.
- S9 (HTTP 402) appears in a search summary saying ramp without landfall payoffs makes a slow deck. Unverified.

## 2. +1/+1 counters

### Takeaway

S5 is the only fetched counters source. It sorts cards into roles, names examples and lists traps, with no recommended counts.

### Cited findings

- Counter sources and enablers: Good-Fortune Unicorn, Innkeeper's Talent, Felidar Retreat ([S5](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/mikecummings-seo-11112025-commander-staples-for-1-1-counter-decks)).
- Doublers: Hardened Scales, Ozolith the Shattered Spire, Branching Evolution. S5 says more exist without giving a count, and calls Hardened Scales essential for green counter builds ([S5](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/mikecummings-seo-11112025-commander-staples-for-1-1-counter-decks)).
- Proliferate: Dreamtide Whale, Evolution Sage, Grateful Apparition, Thrumingbird, and Ripples of Potential ([S5](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/mikecummings-seo-11112025-commander-staples-for-1-1-counter-decks)).
- Payoffs: S5 has no payoff role; its nearest examples are Benthic Biomancer and Fathom Mage ([S5](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/mikecummings-seo-11112025-commander-staples-for-1-1-counter-decks)).
- Evasion: Abzan Falconer, Mer-Ek Nightblade, Duskshell Crawler; Herald of Secret Streams makes countered creatures unblockable ([S5](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/mikecummings-seo-11112025-commander-staples-for-1-1-counter-decks)).
- Other: lifelink for buffed creatures; Inspiring Call (protection and card draw); utility spells Ripples of Potential and Damning Verdict, which S5 expects to be one-sided more than half the time by its own estimate ([S5](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/mikecummings-seo-11112025-commander-staples-for-1-1-counter-decks)).
- Rates: Innkeeper's Talent at Level 1 adds one counter per combat step on your turn; Felidar Retreat yields a 2/2 or a board-wide counter plus vigilance as each land enters; Good-Fortune Unicorn gives each entering creature a counter; Ozolith adds a counter whenever counters are added; Branching Evolution doubles counters placed; Evolution Sage doubles proliferate when a fetchland enters ([S5](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/mikecummings-seo-11112025-commander-staples-for-1-1-counter-decks)).
- Count: none recommended. A tally of the named cards gives 22 distinct cards; that describes the article and is not an author count ([S5](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/mikecummings-seo-11112025-commander-staples-for-1-1-counter-decks)).
- Traps: blockers can stall counters alone; Felidar Retreat's upside depends on it staying in play; the big payoff depends on trigger order; a runaway counter board draws table attention ([S5](https://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/mikecummings-seo-11112025-commander-staples-for-1-1-counter-decks)).
- **Unverified:** a Draftsim ranking puts Marchesa, the Black Rose first and Vorinclex second, framing Vorinclex as a command-zone Doubling Season ([S19](https://draftsim.com/mtg-plus-one-counter-commanders/)). Lae'zel's deck spreads counters through planeswalkers and loops Heliod with Walking Ballista ([S11](https://draftsim.com/laezel-edh-deck/)). Coven Counters' commander adds a counter at the start of combat ([S12](https://infinite.tcgplayer.com/article/Coven-Counters-MTG-Commander-Precon-Upgrade-Guide/4acae650-7627-402e-9de7-5412e5294daa/)). The Counter Intelligence guide names Lux Artillery and Walking Ballista as standout payoffs in a counters-and-artifacts plan ([S13](https://www.tcgplayer.com/content/article/Edge-of-Eternities-Counter-Intelligence-MTG-Commander-Precon-Deck-Upgrade-Guide/e53de224-3630-42af-9f3c-eb37393e27e0/)). That precon is the base of the owner's deck 4 (project file).

### Inferences

- **Inference:** the sources agree on roles (sources, doublers, proliferate, evasion) but give no ratio. Nothing here is a target for enablers against payoffs.
- **Inference:** S5's Felidar Retreat trap argues against resting a plan on one source. S5 does not say this itself.
- **Inference:** S5's evasion and protection cards are its answers to blockers and table attention.

### Gaps

- No enabler, payoff or removal counts; one pod remark only.
- Counters on attack: not sourced. The owner's Seismic Tutelage signal is unverified here.

## 3. Spellslinger and instants

### Takeaway

S4 defines the archetype and its needs, and gives no counts. No fetched source gives instant or sorcery density, cost reducers, draw, copy or protection counts. Card Kingdom's guide returned HTTP 403.

### Cited findings

- Definition: a compact board built from instants and sorceries. Magecraft permanents reward casting or copying them; blue and red hold most of them; copy and recast effects are core strengths of both colors ([S4](https://commandersherald.com/one-theme-three-techs-spellslinger/)).
- Needs: the instant and sorcery engine; payoff permanents or copy and recast effects; a small board that grows; a way to win ([S4](https://commandersherald.com/one-theme-three-techs-spellslinger/)).
- Win condition: S4's only quotation: "So long as you don't forget to include a wincon." It does not single out the commander ([S4](https://commandersherald.com/one-theme-three-techs-spellslinger/)).
- Mana: treasures are the stated mana solution; mana rituals appear without caveats; temporary mana is not discussed ([S4](https://commandersherald.com/one-theme-three-techs-spellslinger/)).
- Protection: the Extus section says protection and disruption are needed for the endgame, with no count ([S4](https://commandersherald.com/one-theme-three-techs-spellslinger/)).
- Not in S4: counts of instants and sorceries, cost reducers, draw, copy effects, mana from spells, protection pieces, or pod play ([S4](https://commandersherald.com/one-theme-three-techs-spellslinger/)).
- **Unverified:** Card Kingdom warns against relying on the commander as the only win condition and shows small swaps that raise the spell count ([S7](https://blog.cardkingdom.com/?p=11565)). Across the spellslinger results, a summary says the leaders reward every instant and sorcery cast, with blue-red holding most of the support ([S18](https://draftsim.com/best-spellslinger-commanders/)). One Draftsim deck gets 17 of its 43 copy targets from adventure cards; that is one list, not a rule ([S10](https://draftsim.com/riku-edh-deck/)).

### Inferences

- **Inference:** S4's four needs are separate functions. Count engine, payoffs, board growth and win condition on their own lines; no source gives numbers for them.
- **Inference:** treasure is permanent mana, so S4's answer does not cover temporary combat mana. That stays an open gap.
- **Owner signal:** his instants deck empties its hand fastest, needs the most card draw, and wanted Horizon Stone to hold combat mana. No source here gives a draw count.

### Gaps

- Density, cost reducer, copy, draw and protection counts: none fetched.
- Not read: Card Kingdom (HTTP 403), Geeky Domain's spellslinger concept guide, Draftsim's 55-commander ranking (S18).

## 4. Graveyard and reanimator

### Takeaway

One fetched source (S3) gives counts: revival enablers usually at 3 mana or less, 8 to 12 expensive creatures (usually 5 to 7 mana), and several revival spells rather than one or two. It gives no reanimation-spell count. Its graveyard-hate advice favors lasting hate and threats that work without the graveyard. Single source; authority not checked.

### Cited findings

- Core pieces: creatures worth returning, effects that return them, and a steady way to fill the graveyard ([S3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-edh-reanimator-deck)).
- Enablers: usually 3 mana or less and almost never above 5. Entomb (1 mana), Buried Alive (3 mana; three creatures into the graveyard), Faithless Looting (1 red; draws 2, discards 2, with flashback). No self-mill count ([S3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-edh-reanimator-deck)).
- Reanimation: Reanimate (1 black; pay life equal to the creature's mana value), Animate Dead (2 mana, Aura, any graveyard), Victimize (3 mana; sacrifice one creature to return two). The article says a single Reanimate belongs in any black graveyard deck ([S3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-edh-reanimator-deck)).
- Targets: 8 to 12 expensive creatures, usually costing 5 to 7 mana ([S3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-edh-reanimator-deck)).
- Reanimation count: none given. Advice is to run several revival spells and extra tutors for redundancy ([S3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-edh-reanimator-deck)).
- Commanders named: Meren of Clan Nel Toth, Muldrotha, Karador, The Scarab God. Reanimator almost always includes black ([S3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-edh-reanimator-deck)).
- Graveyard hate: the opposing answers named are Relic of Progenitus, Tormod's Crypt and Leyline of the Void; Containment Priest and Grafdigger's Cage limit creatures entering. Against reanimator, lasting hate and exile beat bounce. Keep threats that work without the graveyard so that exile does not end the plan ([S3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-edh-reanimator-deck)).
- Pods: Gray Merchant can finish the game at high devotion; the archetype punishes slow play; Reanimate can target any graveyard, including an opponent's ([S3](https://www.nerdleagues.com/blog/how-to-build-an-edh-reanimator-deck)).

### Inferences

- **Inference:** a threat that works without the graveyard is a separate function; count it on its own line.
- **Inference:** S3's advice to run several revival spells agrees with the owner's experience that two never worked. Neither gives a number.

### Gaps

- Enabler, reanimation-spell and self-mill counts beyond S3: none.
- Not read: a Draftsim reanimator ranking (dated March 12, 2026 in its listing) and Star City Games' legacy-style reanimator deck tech.

## 5. Blink and enter-the-battlefield (brief)

### Takeaway

No fetched source. Search summaries only, all unverified. They describe pairing enter-the-battlefield creatures with re-entry effects and warn against too many flicker effects.

### Cited findings (all unverified)

- Blink (flicker) sends permanents away and back to re-trigger enter-the-battlefield effects ([S15](https://geekydomain.com/blogs/guides/best-budget-mtg-blink-flicker-deck-under-100-2026)).
- A lopsided mix underperforms; creatures without such an effect generally miss the cut; newer builders overload flicker ([S15](https://geekydomain.com/blogs/guides/best-budget-mtg-blink-flicker-deck-under-100-2026), [S16](https://krakenthemeta.com/blog/?p=1338)). The summaries do not say which page makes each point; the links name candidate pages only.
- Backup blink: Conjurer's Closet and Teleportation Circle (search summary; source not identified). Soulherder as a cheap repeated blink (budget-guide summary, [S15](https://geekydomain.com/blogs/guides/best-budget-mtg-blink-flicker-deck-under-100-2026)).
- Commanders named in the summaries: Brago, King Eternal; Yarok, the Desecrated (doubles enter-the-battlefield triggers) ([S16](https://krakenthemeta.com/blog/?p=1338)); Aminatou, the Fateshifter ([S17](https://draftsim.com/aminatou-edh-deck/)); Gilraen, Dúnedain Protector (source not identified in the search summary).

### Gaps

- No counts; no pod evidence. Not read: Draftsim's 2026 blink ranking (27 commanders).

## 6. Aristocrats and sacrifice (brief)

### Takeaway

Every count here comes from a summary or one low-authority site, so all are unverified. The TCGplayer guide returned no text, and Card Kingdom returned HTTP 403.

### Cited findings (all unverified)

- Roles: cheap bodies to feed, repeatable sacrifice outlets, payoffs that reward each death ([S6](https://www.tcgplayer.com/content/article/How-to-Play-an-Aristocrats-Commander-Deck/61620389-aef6-4c14-93ae-2a3645173f6c/)).
- Outlets: the summary calls them the most crucial piece, suggests about seven to twelve slots, and advises a spare outlet before any kill attempt. A commander that can act as an outlet helps (Yawgmoth, Thran Physician is named). A failed combo makes you the table's target ([S6](https://www.tcgplayer.com/content/article/How-to-Play-an-Aristocrats-Commander-Deck/61620389-aef6-4c14-93ae-2a3645173f6c/)).
- Count from a low-authority site: six to eight zero-cost sacrifice outlets, fifteen to twenty noncreature spells, eight to ten removal spells ([S14](https://edh-match.vercel.app/strategies/aristocrats)). These outlet counts measure different things.
- Card Kingdom's checklist, per its summary, starts with sacrifice enablers and favors several distinct cards per role in singleton formats ([S8](https://blog.cardkingdom.com/?p=8045)).

### Gaps

- No fetched aristocrats composition source; the next candidates are in the Gaps list.

- Reanimator: enablers; reanimation effects; targets; threats outside the graveyard; graveyard-hate defense (S3).
- Aristocrats: fodder, outlets, payoffs (S6, unverified). Blink: enter-the-battlefield creatures and re-entry effects (S15, unverified).

## Conflicts between sources

1. **Ramp definition.** S1 excludes mana rocks; S2 counts land fetch, rocks and creatures ([S1](https://articles.starcitygames.com/articles/commander-fundamentals-ramp/), [S2](http://www.coolstuffinc.com/a/markwischkaemper-06222023-how-much-ramp-is-right-for-your-commander-deck)). Numbers from one are not comparable with the other.
2. **Land baselines.** 37 (S1 sample), 38 to 40 (S3), 40 plus 8 to 10 ramp (S2), 38 to 42 with rocks (S2, citing Karsten). These are different deck types; none is a landfall baseline.
3. **Surplus ramp.** S2 says more mana generally helps, so lean higher, and also that near-dead ramp is a risk. S1 says ramp stops being ramp past about five mana. S2's own condition reconciles them: the deck must be able to use the mana.
4. **Aristocrats outlet counts.** S6 (summary) gives seven to twelve outlet slots; S14 gives six to eight zero-cost outlets. Different measures; both unverified.
5. **Age.** S1 (2018) predates much of the current card pool, so its counts may not hold for current cards.
6. **Temporary mana.** S4 treats treasure as the mana answer; the owner's games needed a mana-retention artifact. This is a gap in the sources, not a conflict between them.
